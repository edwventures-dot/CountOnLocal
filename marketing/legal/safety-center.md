Count On Local lets neighbors hire local people for small, recurring jobs — bins to
the curb, a dog walked, a yard tidied. Some of the people offering those jobs are
teenagers, aged 13 to 17. Because of that, we've written down exactly how this works,
what we check, and — just as importantly — what we don't. Please read it. It's short.

---

## The honest part first: what we check, and what we don't

We want you to trust this, so we're not going to overstate it.

**We do NOT run background checks.** When you see "Identity verified," it means our
payments provider (Stripe) confirmed a payment identity — nothing more. We will never
call that a background check, and we don't describe anyone as a "safe provider,"
"fully vetted," or "guaranteed safe." Those words would be a promise we haven't
earned, so we don't use them.

**What we actually verify:**
- Customers confirm a working email and phone number, and provide a valid payment
  method, before any paid job.
- Providers meet our payment provider's identity requirements before they can be paid.
- A badge only appears where it's true — "Guardian connected" shows only when a
  guardian is genuinely verified.

Trust here is built on honesty about the limits, plus the rules below.

## How the guardian connection works (for providers aged 13–17)

A teenager cannot take a single paying customer until a parent or legal guardian is
connected and verified. Until then, they can build and save a draft, but they can't
publish a paid service or accept money.

**The guardian holds the money.** For a provider under 18, the payout account is held
by their verified guardian until the provider turns 18. The teen earns; the guardian
receives and oversees the payouts.

**A guardian can withdraw approval at any time.** The moment they do:
1. the provider's business is paused,
2. no new customers can subscribe,
3. future charges stop,
4. any work that was already paid for is handed to our support team (and the guardian)
   to resolve safely rather than simply dropped.

## What jobs are allowed — and what aren't

Not every job is open to a teenager, and a provider can't quietly change what a job
is. The service list controls this: someone offering "manual yard cleanup" cannot turn
it into "chainsaw tree trimming" by rewriting the description.

**Generally allowed (lower-risk, outdoor):** trash cans to the curb, outdoor watering,
moving in packages, picking up leaves and sticks by hand.

**Allowed with guardian approval and extra care:** things like dog walking or dog-waste
pickup — with the safety details below.

**Not allowed for minors:** ladders, chainsaws or powered cutting tools, heavy
machinery, pesticides or regulated chemicals, and anything using a customer's hazardous
tools. In-home, licensed, or high-trust jobs aren't part of the minor experience.

## Privacy and addresses

- A provider's **home address, school, date of birth, exact age, and personal schedule
  are never public** and never shown on a map.
- A **customer's service address** is seen only by that customer, the provider actually
  assigned to the job, the guardian of a teen provider, and a small number of audited
  staff. It is never returned by search and never shown publicly.
- **Gate or access codes** are treated as sensitive: never in emails, notification
  previews, or logs.
- Public pages show only a general **neighborhood or area** — never a pin on where a
  provider lives or works.

We also don't collect what we don't need — no school name, no class schedule, no live
GPS history.

## Where the money goes

- The provider keeps **100% of their listed price** — there is no fee taken from the
  provider.
- The **customer pays a platform fee on top** (a percentage with a small per-cycle
  minimum).
- Subscriptions are billed each cycle in advance. Customers can **pause, skip a visit,
  or cancel** themselves. Cancelling refunds the unused part of the cycle; pausing keeps
  your credit rather than refunding it.
- Payments and payouts run through **Stripe**. Stripe holds card details — **we never
  see or store a card number.**
- For a provider under 18, the payout account is held by their guardian until they
  turn 18 (see above).

## Dogs and animals

For dog walking, the customer tells us the dog's count, size, leash/harness, any
bite history, behavior notes, and an emergency contact. Providers set their own limits,
and we won't match a dog outside them. We can suspend a dog or a service after a safety
report.

## Messaging and contact

Messaging only exists inside an actual job relationship, and **block and report are
always available.** A teen provider's direct email and phone aren't shown by default.
Our systems watch for attempts to move payment or contact off the platform, threats, or
inappropriate content, and flag them for review.

## Reporting a concern — and what happens next

If something feels wrong, report it. _[Describe how — in-app report button + a contact
address — confirm before publish.]_

We sort reports by seriousness:
- **Emergency / immediate threat** — see the note below.
- **Physical safety, harassment, a credible threat, a missing animal, serious property
  damage** — highest priority.
- **Repeated boundary-crossing, an unsafe instruction, or a payment/fraud problem.**
- **Ordinary quality or service disputes.**

**Safety reports jump the queue** ahead of normal support. We can pause an account or a
business immediately, hold a payout where our policy allows, and — when a minor is
involved and it's safe and lawful to do so — notify their guardian. Every action our
staff takes is logged, with who did it and why. We don't publicly share the private
details of an incident.

**Count On Local is not an emergency service.** If someone is in danger right now, call
your local emergency number (911 in the US). We can't dispatch help.

## Things we deliberately haven't built

We keep the product narrow on purpose. Count On Local does **not** currently do, and
would treat as a whole new safety review before ever adding: under-13 users, childcare,
in-home jobs by minors, live location tracking, driving/transportation, medical or
elder care, or handling student data through a school.
