# Count On Local Brand Guide

## Brand idea

**Count On Local** is where a small neighborhood service becomes a real business.

The brand should respect young providers as owners. It is not a chore chart, a teen employment agency, or a charity program.

## Primary tagline

**Start a business where you live.**

## Supporting lines

Provider:
- `Turn what you can do into something neighbors subscribe to.`
- `Your service. Your price. Your customers.`
- `Start small. Build local.`

Customer:
- `Good help is closer than you think.`
- `Subscribe to reliable help from someone nearby.`
- `Small jobs. Local people. One less thing to remember.`

Guardian:
- `They run the business. You stay connected.`
- `Real earning, with guardian visibility built in.`

## Positioning statement

For motivated young people who want to earn independently, Count On Local is a neighborhood microbusiness platform that provides a professional storefront, recurring customers, payments, route tools, and guardian support. Unlike job boards that make workers wait for gigs, Count On Local helps providers create the offer first and build a small business around repeat neighborhood demand.

## Brand promise

**If you are willing to do useful work consistently, Count On Local gives you the tools to sell it like a real business.**

## Audience hierarchy

### 1. Provider - primary growth engine
Motivations: money, independence, proof they can build something, flexible schedule, ownership.

Do not talk down to them. Avoid "learn responsibility" as the lead message; that is parent language.

### 2. Customer - revenue engine
Motivations: reliability, convenience, local trust, affordable recurring help, support local young people as a bonus.

Lead with the solved problem, not guilt or charity.

### 3. Guardian - permission/trust engine
Motivations: safety, visibility, practical experience, controlled independence, legitimate payments.

## Voice

Count On Local is:

- direct;
- upbeat;
- concise;
- capable;
- lightly clever;
- never corporate for the sake of sounding important.

### Say
`Trash day is every week. Your customer should be too.`

`You already do the work. Give it a real page.`

`Eight houses on one street beats eight houses across town.`

### Avoid
`Empowering youth through a transformational gig ecosystem.`

`Unlock your entrepreneurial journey!`

`Kids can learn responsibility while earning allowance.`

## Naming rules inside the product

- User-facing provider identity is `business` or `provider`, not `tasker`.
- Customer purchases a `service` or `plan`, not a `gig`.
- Recurring commercial relationship is `subscription` in account/billing screens; public marketing may say `weekly service` when more natural.
- Operational instances are `services` in UI and `occurrences` internally.
- `Guardian` is preferred over `parent` because legal guardian may not be a parent.

## Visual identity

### Palette

- Ink `#14263A`: serious enough for parents/customers.
- Lime `#C7F34A`: energetic action color.
- Cream `#F6F3EA`: warm neighborhood background.
- Coral `#FF765C`: limited secondary energy.
- White `#FFFFFF`: card surface.

### Typography

Manrope for brand/heading. Inter for body/UI.

### Photography

Prefer real providers doing the service, with guardian/customer consent. Show competence and context: moving bins, walking a dog, raking, handing over a clean result.

Avoid:

- staged stock-photo high-fives;
- school hallway imagery;
- cash fan photos;
- imagery that exposes a minor's house/school/address;
- hero shots that make the provider look like a child being supervised.

### Illustration

Use route lines, service cards, simplified houses/blocks, checkmarks, receipts, calendars. Keep illustrative geography abstract.

## Logo concept

Count On Local's mark combines a route-like `S` and a forward endpoint. It represents starting locally and building momentum.

Minimum clear space: height of the mark's endpoint circle on all sides.

Do not recolor the Lime mark with random gradients.

## Trust language

The brand earns trust by being specific.

Use factual labels:

- Guardian connected
- Identity verified
- Payments protected
- X completed services

Do not promise:

- "safe" as an absolute;
- "fully vetted" unless that exact process exists;
- a background check based on identity verification.

## Core campaign platform

### Campaign: `Start on your street.`

Provider creative should show ridiculous-but-real small businesses:

- `$3/week: I bring your bins back.`
- `$8/week: I pick up the dog poop.`
- `$10/week: I water the porch plants.`

The point is not glamorous work. The point is turning overlooked recurring annoyance into recurring revenue.

## Brand architecture

Count On Local is consumer-facing. EDW Ventures appears in legal/footer/corporate contexts, not as the main customer brand.
